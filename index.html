Why this one

Snowbound ships as two runtime modules: SnowboundWeather (director, sky, seasons, zones, rain/status/wind components) and SnowboundSnow (the Deformable Snow Kit: render-target snow deformation plus geometric snow accumulation), plus the editor-only SnowboundWeatherEditor (the Set Up Level wizard, editable weather presets, Project Settings pages, and the cook rule that packages the runtime content). It supports Unreal Engine 5.5, 5.6, 5.7 and 5.8 on Win64.

  • Multiplayer that actually works — server-authoritative state machine, deterministic seeded transitions, late-join sync (weather, time of day, season, surface wetness, ice), 2 Hz coarse replication with local reconstruction. Drop it into a listen-server game and every client sees the same storm.
  • Weather as gameplay — replicated squall smoke-screens with a sight-blocking query (IsSightBlockedBySquall) for AI, aim and UI; a Beaufort-style severity API with a playability cap; per-location weather queries; wind hooks for projectiles. Squalls respect weather zones (a calm valley spawns none; a rain zone inside a snowstorm gets rain curtains), can carry a spatialized howl (SquallSound), white the camera out while you stand inside one, and a rain curtain expiring in freezing air leaves a flash-ice trail down its drift lane (bSquallFlashIce).
  • A storm that escalates — past a severity threshold the zero-setup snowfall hands off to a dedicated wind-torn blizzard system (AutoBlizzardSystem, hysteretic swap, cross-faded), so a full gale reads as a whiteout, not as “more snowfall” — and while the tier is engaged the wind bed plays louder and gusts shake the camera harder. Opt-in: wire the blizzard system’s wind link, then set BlizzardSeverityThreshold (ships at 1.0 = off).
  • A season cycle you can watch happen — the winter↔summer axis drives the whole world: the blanket melts in islands off sun-facing slopes, ponds thin to slush and open, trees bud and leaf out, grass colours in, puddles collect, footsteps turn from snow to dirt and splashes — then autumn turns the leaves and the first flurries whiten the ground again. What falls follows the air temperature, with a sleet band between. Deterministic and replicated, so every client sees the same melt pattern. One flag pins the ground to winter for a permanently wintry game. (details)
  • Winter specialization — ground-snow baseline and accumulation hooks (IBOSWeatherGroundResponder), freezing-rain ice events, whiteout model.
  • Precipitation that scales to the machine — falling snow and rain as world Niagara or as a screen-space parallax sheet (one full-screen pass at any particle count), chosen from Effects Quality or by the player and switchable mid-storm; quality tiers up to an uncapped Cinematic, plus a Sequencer/photo-mode override stack. (details)
  • A snow ground that remembers — footprints, impact craters, slide trails, glazed ice and wind-sculpted drifts in a shared render target, plus geometric snow piles that grow on props during a storm — all deterministic across the network with zero per-deposit bandwidth.
  • Localized weather — BOSWeatherZone volumes blend viewer-local weather (fog, post, precipitation, wind, audio) — a calm valley inside a blizzard — with deterministic per-location queries for game logic.
  • A sky that lives — volumetric clouds that drift at real speeds under a sky time-lapse, reshape per weather state and can be parked behind a landmark, a high cirrus layer that shears against the deck, a moonlit night deck, physically lit days with HDR exposure, and a procedural night sky — all replicated, so every player sees the same clouds in the same place. (details)
  • Set up in one click — Tools → Snowbound: Set Up Level places or repairs the director, sun, sky light, atmosphere, fog and weather-reactive clouds; Create Editable Weather Presets writes every built-in look as an asset to tweak. (details)
  • In-editor preview — see any state at any hour live in the viewport, no PIE (Details panel → Editor Preview; auto-stops on level save).

Quick start (5 minutes)

  1. Enable the plugin, restart the editor.
  2. Tools → Snowbound: Set Up Level places or repairs everything a weather level needs, in one undoable step that is saved with the map: a Weather Director, a Movable sun at 75 000 lux that is the atmosphere's sun light, a Movable sky light with real-time capture, a SkyAtmosphere, an Exponential Height Fog and a VolumetricCloud with the weather-reactive cloud material. Existing actors are kept and only fixed where needed (an existing cloud keeps its material — see step 4). A notification lists what it changed. Skip to step 5.
  3. By hand instead: place a Snowbound Weather Director and assign your level's Directional Light, Sky Light, Exponential Height Fog, Sky Atmosphere, Volumetric Cloud actors in its Details panel (unassigned ones are auto-discovered — the first actor of each class in the level).
  4. Cloud material: nothing to do — when the cloud's material does not read the weather collection (the engine default m_SimpleVolumetricCloud_Inst renders a static sky), the Director assigns /SnowboundWeather/Materials/MI_VolumetricCloud automatically, the moment you assign the actor in the Details panel (persists with the level) or at BeginPlay for auto-discovered clouds. All cloud weather (coverage, density, storm decks, weather fronts, wind pan) lives in that material via MPC_Weather. Prefer to KEEP your own styled or third-party sky material? Set the Director's Cloud Material Integration to Drive the existing material's parameters: your material is kept and wrapped in a dynamic instance, and the Director pushes coverage / density / storm / wind into the parameter names you pick (CustomCloud*Param, defaults CloudCoverage / CloudDensity / StormClouds / WindVec) — parameters your material lacks are simply ignored. Warn only restores the old behaviour, and a material that already samples the weather collection is never touched in any mode.
  5. Optional: assign per-state BOSWeatherPreset DataAssets (Presets map); states without one use built-in defaults. Tools → Snowbound: Create Editable Weather Presets writes one preset per built-in state (Clear … Thunderstorm) into /Game/Snowbound/WeatherPresets, pre-filled with the built-in tuning, and assigns them to the level's Director — so you tweak the shipped look instead of rebuilding it. Any preset asset can be reset the same way (Template State + Load Built-In Values). Edits to a preset show immediately in PIE and in the editor weather preview. Optional: point your materials at /SnowboundWeather/MPC_Weather — or point the plugin at YOUR collection in Project Settings → Plugins → Snowbound Weather (saved to DefaultGame.ini): ini [/Script/SnowboundWeather.BOSWeatherSubsystem] WeatherMPC=/Game/Path/To/YourMPC.YourMPC
  6. Play. The sun already cycles (day/night is on by default, a ten-minute day - see Day / night cycle). Drive it from code/BP: SetWeatherState, SetWeatherSchedule, SetSeasonLoop, SetTimeOfDayTarget, ApplyHostOverrides (lobby picks), zone volumes, severity queries.

Or open /SnowboundWeather/Maps/Lvl_SnowboundDemo first: a fully wired level (director, landscape with the demo snow material, weather zones, a frozen pond, obstacle crates that collect snow piles) plus a runtime weather control panel (BOSWeatherControlPanelActor) you can also drop into your own map to flip states, hours and seasons while testing.

Physical sun + Lumen: the presets drive a physically scaled sun (75 000 lux by day), so a white snow scene sits at EV 12–15 and the engine will warn that "Cached lighting in Lumen … is going to be clipped" with the default pre-exposure. Add to your DefaultEngine.ini: ini [SystemSettings] r.EyeAdaptation.CachedLightingPreExposure=8 ; engine-recommended full physical range [-4, 16]

Gameplay integration

Everything below derives from already-replicated inputs or level-static data, so the server and every client compute the same answer — safe for game logic, not just for HUDs.

  • Temperature — GetTemperatureCelsiusAtLocation / GetTemperatureFahrenheit / IsFreezingAtLocation / GetTemperatureBandAtLocation. Three layers: the season axis plus a diurnal swing; the active weather’s TemperatureOffsetC (lerps through transitions and blends through weather zones for free); and additive BOSTemperatureSourceComponent point sources for campfires, forges and cold cellars — plus an altitude lapse rate. Published to MPC_Weather as Temperature for frost and breath-fog materials. Adds no replicated state.
  • Actor weather status — drop a BOSWeatherStatusComponent on any actor and it reports the weather, severity and temperature where that actor stands, whether it is under cover (IsOutdoors / GetShelterFactor, a throttled CPU trace), and how soaked it has become (GetExposureWetness) — with dispatchers for shelter, local-weather and temperature-band changes so you bind events instead of polling. Purely local, nothing replicated.
  • Wind — GetWindVectorAtLocation returns the effective wind including the gust envelope and any zone override. BOSWindForceComponent is the worked example: it pushes an actor’s simulating bodies downwind.
  • Time of day — OnHourChanged, OnSunrise, OnSunset, OnDayNightChanged and data-driven TimeTriggers → OnTimeTrigger(Tag). Sunrise and sunset follow the real sun altitude rather than fixed hours. They fire on every machine, so client-side lights and VFX react without any replication of their own, and a clock jump (SetTimeOfDay, a host override, the first replication) does not machine-gun the events it skipped — see bFireEventsOnTimeJump.
  • Snow depth — GetSnowDepthAtPosition on the deformation subsystem folds the render-target state and the geometric piles into one 0..1 answer, so movement drag, footstep muffling or an item economy can read “how deep am I?” with a single call.

Ambient weather without an authored timeline

Set bEnableWeatherRandomizer and the server rolls new states from a weighted, optionally season-biased distribution (FBOSWeatherRandomizerConfig) — seeded, so a QA session is reproducible. Driver precedence is explicit: a manual SetWeatherStateManual pick beats a running schedule, which beats the randomizer, which beats the season→weather coupling. Rolls end in an ordinary SetWeatherState, so nothing new goes on the wire and late joiners are already handled. Default off — modes that drive weather deliberately must not have it changed underneath them.

Save games

CaptureWeatherState() returns one FBOSWeatherSaveState — weather state and blend position, time of day and clock mode / day length, the full season config plus the authored loop range, surface wetness, the schedule playhead, the star seed, the randomizer’s stream position, the wind override, the runtime Custom preset, the season-row switches, a manual weather hold and the snow state (accumulation level + pile reveal progress: piles regrow deterministically from the seed, so no geometry is stored). Put it in your USaveGame; RestoreWeatherState() puts it back, tells every OnWeatherStateChanged listener and forces an immediate replication sync. The struct carries a Version (now 3); v1/v2 snapshots still restore and leave the parts they lack untouched.

A restore is a snapshot, not a suggestion. After a load the world stays where the save left it until the next command: a held manual pick stays held, a paused clock stays paused, a running cycle keeps sweeping the saved range, piles reappear rather than regrow. The season→weather coupling fires again only at the next band crossing.

  • Partial loads — RestoreWeatherStateEx(State, Mask) / LoadWeatherFromSlotEx take an EBOSWeatherRestoreMask bit set (Sky, Time, Season, Snow, Wind, Randomizer, Schedule, Flags; 255 = all) and return an EBOSWeatherSaveResult — a refused load is never a silent false. OnWeatherRestoredDispatcher fires with slot, mask and result.
  • Slots — UBOSWeatherSaveLibrary: save / load / list / info / delete / exists, JSON export and import. @ is a legal slot character.
  • Persistence, zero setup — bAutoSaveOnEndPlay (+ periodic AutoSaveIntervalSeconds) and AutoLoadSlotName are Config (project default in DefaultGame.ini) and overridden per user by SetAutoSaveOnExit / SetAutoLoadSlot (the panel’s “Remember” row). Auto slots are <name>@<Map> (bAutoSlotsPerMap), only a slot saved on this map auto-loads (bAutoLoadRequiresSameMap), and ShouldAutoLoadNow lets a game veto (explicit host picks win). QuickSave / QuickLoad use QuickSave@<Map>.
  • Console — snowbound.Weather.Save | Load | List | Delete | Export | Import | QuickSave | QuickLoad; snowbound.Weather.TraceRestore 1 logs every weather-state change with its driver (on automatically for 10 s after a restore).
  • Not saved, on purpose — footprints, craters, glaze, water-body freeze state, per-user precip / lens settings, zone enable flags.

Day / night cycle

On by default: a Director drives the sun angle and a day/night light curve from its clock, and a fresh level loops a full 24 h day in DayLengthSeconds (600 = a ten-minute day). The knobs live under Snowbound > Weather > DayNight on the Director:

  • bDayNightEnabled - the master switch. Off = the sun keeps the rotation and intensity it was placed with and nothing below applies. Replicated; SetDayNightEnabled flips it at runtime.
  • TimeOfDayMode - FullCycle (default) loops 0..24 h, Ranged sweeps StartHour..EndHour (bPingPong bounces instead of snapping back), Static holds StartHour.
  • StartHour / EndHour, bRandomizeStartHour (each match opens at a different hour), SunYawAxis / SunNoonPitch (where the sun rises and how high it climbs), the optional MoonLightActor (a SECOND Movable DirectionalLight for the night side), and the light curve knobs (night floor, twilight band, sky-light multipliers).

What the level needs: a Movable DirectionalLight (the Director forces Movable at runtime and warns; Stationary/Static lights silently reject rotation), a sky light (forced Movable and put on real-time capture so ambient and cloud lighting follow the sky every frame), and a sun intensity near 75 000 lux - the curve scales relative to the placed intensity, so a 6-lux sun cycles between dim and dimmer.

Runtime (authority, replicated): SetTimeOfDay (jump), SetTimeOfDayTarget (sweep to an hour and hold), SetDayNightCycleRunning (pause / resume the loop from the current hour), SetDayLength* (speed without moving the sun), SetDayNightEnabled. Calling any of the first three on a level that left the driver off switches it on - the control panel's Cycle box and day-length ladder rely on that. Sunrise / sunset / hour events: see Events & hooks.

Day lighting & HDR exposure

The day side of the cycle is lit physically, not by a fixed color ramp. Knobs under Snowbound > Weather > DayNight > Lighting (all identity while bDayNightEnabled is off):

  • bSunColorFromAtmosphere (on) + AtmosphereSunTintStrength (0.8) - the sun's color comes from the level Sky Atmosphere's transmittance for the current sun direction: white at noon, golden through sunset (a built-in perceptual soften plus the 0.8 default keep the horizon hue golden rather than physically deep red; raise toward 1 for the raw hue). No Sky Atmosphere in the level (or knob off) falls back to the built-in analytic golden-hour tint.
  • SunsetFadeStartDeg (10) / NightFullAltitudeDeg (-8) - two-phase dusk: DIRECT sunlight fades from 10° altitude to zero at the horizon, while ambient (sky light, fog, night sky, moon, exposure) holds until geometric sunset and then eases to full night at -8° (civil+nautical twilight) - light lingers after sundown instead of slamming to night.
  • TwilightTimeScale (0.5) - the clock runs at half speed through the twilight band (raised-cosine, peak dwell near the horizon), so sunset and dawn stop racing on short game days; 1 = off. The full day then takes slightly longer in real time than DayLengthSeconds. Clients follow automatically via the replicated clock rate.
  • bDriveSkyLightColor (on) + NightSkyLightTint (0.60, 0.70, 1.00) - the sky light's color blends from the authored color by day toward a cool blue-hour tint at night, so snow reads moonlit instead of grey. The authored color is captured at BeginPlay and restored by the editor preview's Stop.
  • FogSunsetTint (0.6) - height-fog inscattering warms toward the live sun color while the sun is low (sunset haze); 0 keeps the preset fog color at all hours.
  • NightFogColorScale (0.12) - fog color brightness at deep night. Preset fog colors are authored for daylight; without this, fog glows grey at midnight.
  • bDayExposureClamp (off) + DayAutoExposureMinEV (10) / DayAutoExposureMaxEV (17) - the daytime counterpart of bNightExposureClamp: clamps the EV100 auto-exposure window in daylight so a bright HDR sky neither greys out nor crushes the foreground. Sun-lit snow meters at EV100 ≈ 15-17, so keep the ceiling at or above that - a lower ceiling forces the camera brighter than the scene and blows the whites out. Both clamps compose into one window lerped across the cycle; a side whose knob is off keeps the engine's wide-open default there. On a fully auto-created scene (the Director had to spawn the sun and the level has no PostProcessVolume) both clamps arm automatically for the session.
  • bDriveExposureBias (off) + ExposureBiasDay / ExposureBiasNight (0 / 0) - drives post-process exposure compensation across the cycle: one-knob day/night mood grading, independent of the window clamps.
  • bSkyLightRealTimeCapture (on) - the sky light's "HDR mode": per-frame real-time capture while day/night runs, so ambient and volumetric-cloud lighting follow the live sky continuously. Off = the cheap throttled 5 s recapture fallback for low-end targets (dawn/dusk lighting steps return). A capture the level authored on is never forced off.
  • SkyLightDayBoost (1) - extra ambient multiplier at full day, fading to ×1 at night: lifts midday ambient on snow without touching the authored night floor.
  • bDriveLowerHemisphere (off) + LowerHemisphereDayColor / LowerHemisphereNightScale (0.05) - ground-bounce term: snow reflects most of the sky, but the engine default bounces black from below. Opt-in because it visibly brightens the shaded side of everything.

  • bAutoFixLowSunIntensity (off) - repairs the "always dark sky" trap: a sun authored far below the ~75 000-lux physical reference (< 2 000 lux) is raised to ReferenceSunIntensity at startup instead of only logging a warning. A softer log line also flags suns outside the plausible 20 000-200 000-lux daylight band.

  • bDriveAtmosphereScattering (on) - the Sky Atmosphere's Rayleigh scattering and aerial-perspective view distance follow the weather and season: deep-blue crisp air with sharp far ridges under a clear winter sky, grey washed-out light with close distance haze in storms/rain/fog. The per-preset RayleighScale / AerialPerspectiveScale values are multipliers on the level's authored atmosphere (presets left at 1.0 change nothing).

The Director also pushes the final day-lit sun color each tick as the SunTintDay MPC vector (RGB = tint, A = day factor) and, in DriveCustomParams cloud integration, to the CustomCloudSunTintParam material parameter (default SunTint) - hooks for sunset-colored cloud decks in both the plugin and kept third-party cloud materials.

Night sky

The procedural dome material M_NightSkyProc draws stars, the Milky Way, a phase-correct moon and a 22° ice halo, driven per tick by the Director (celestial rotation, weather extinction, replicated star seed - every client sees the identical sky). Look tuning is scalar params on the material / an MI: StarBrightness (2), StarDensity, StarTwinkle, MilkyWayIntensity (0.25), MoonMariaContrast, MoonHaloStrength (0.10) / MoonHaloWidth (0.004), and UseProceduralMoon - a 0/1 mode switch (1 = the built-in phase moon; 0 is only for projects that draw their own moon dome - fractional values just dim the moon and leave a dark star-mask hole).

Per-layer exposure trims (EV stops, 0 = neutral, +1 = twice as bright): StarExposureEV, MilkyWayExposureEV, MoonExposureEV, and SkyExposureEV for the whole dome - trim layers against each level's exposure setup instead of re-tuning the base look.

Director knobs (Snowbound > Weather > DayNight > Night):

  • MoonAngularSizeDeg (4.5) - apparent moon size; the real moon is 0.5°, 3-8° reads "game-pretty", larger goes blurry.
  • bMoonlightFromPhase (on) + MoonlightLuxFull (0.25) / MoonlightLuxMin (0.02) - the moon light's intensity follows the moon's phase (full = MoonlightLuxFull, half ≈ half of it) and its altitude (horizon extinction), floored at MoonlightLuxMin so a new-moon night stays playable. Off = the flat legacy MoonIntensity.
  • bNightExposureClamp (off) + NightAutoExposureMinEV (-4) / NightAutoExposureMaxEV (1) - clamps the camera's EV100 auto-exposure window as night falls. Without it, eye adaptation over a dark starfield either brightens the sky into structureless grey or - with a bright foreground - crushes the stars to black. Opt in on levels that showcase the night sky; daylight is untouched either way.

Clouds: reshape any preset, move the deck

Every weather state's clouds can be reshaped without touching its preset asset, and the whole deck can be slid or re-formed in place. Everything is owned by the Director, server-authoritative, replicated, and saved with the weather (snapshot v4, part of the Sky restore bit).

  • Per-preset cloud shape - CloudShapeOverrides (Snowbound > Weather > Clouds > Shape): one FBOSCloudShape per state; only its ticked fields replace the preset's values (coverage, density, storm deck, contrast/puffiness, vertical growth, towers, base altitude, thickness, the four species weights and their mix strength), so an override that only raises Overcast's towers keeps following the preset for everything else. Runtime: SetPresetCloudShape, SetPresetCloudShapeField(State, Field, Value), CopyPresetCloudShape(From, To), ResetPresetCloudShape / ResetAllPresetCloudShapes, GetPresetCloudShape (effective) and GetPresetCloudShapeOverride. Changes on screen morph in at CloudShapeGlideSpeed instead of popping. Custom (Code/BP) is excluded - its params already are the custom values.
  • Placement (Clouds > Placement): CloudShiftKm slides the deck and its ground shadows on top of the wind drift (world X/Y, km) - author it to park a cloud bank behind a landmark; CloudReshape (0..1, wraps) re-forms the fine cloud shapes in place, a shape seed. Runtime: SetCloudShift, SetCloudReshape, ResetCloudPlacement (glide at CloudPlacementGlideSpeed, or bInstant). The high cirrus layer is not moved.
  • Sky speed: SetCloudTimeScale (0 = the clouds stand still, 1 = real time, default 6).
  • Control panel - the CLOUDS section: pick the preset ("On screen" follows the weather), sliders for the shape (orange = overridden), Shape from buttons (the built-in Wisps / Cumulus / Deck / Towers / Storm clouds), Reset / Reset all, Move X / Move Y, Re-form + Center, and Sky speed + Still.
  • Console: snowbound.Clouds.Shape <State> <Field> <Value>, .Copy <From> <To>, .Shift <xKm> <yKm> [instant], .Reshape <0..1>, .Speed <scale>, .Reset [State|shapes|placement|all], .Dump.

In multiplayer the clients also adopt the host's accumulated cloud drift and sky speed, so a deck the host parked behind the castle sits there for everyone - including late joiners.

Cloud motion, layers and cost

Director knobs (Snowbound > Weather > Clouds):

  • Physical drift (bCloudDriftPhysical, on): the deck drifts at real speeds — CloudDriftCalmMps (2 m/s at zero wind), plus CloudDriftWindMps (12) at full wind and CloudDriftGustMps (4) at full gust. Turn it off to hand-tune the raw CloudPan*Rate units.
  • Sky time-lapse (CloudTimeScale, 6): drift and shape evolution run this many times faster than real time while the day clock, seasons and schedule are untouched — 1 is strictly realistic, 4–12 reads "alive but believable". bCloudTimeFollowsDayCycle additionally multiplies it by the day cycle's time compression (capped at CloudTimeScaleMax, 30).
  • High cirrus (bEnableCirrusLayer, on): a cheap translucent dome above the volumetric deck, panned slower (CirrusPanScale 0.4) and on a veered heading (CirrusShearAngleDeg 20°) so the two layers visibly shear. It fades out as coverage closes past ~0.65, is spawned at BeginPlay and never saved into the map. CirrusLayerMaterial takes your own sky layer.
  • Moonlit clouds (bMoonLightsClouds, on): the driven moon light becomes the atmosphere's second sun, so the night deck receives moonlight instead of going pitch black.
  • Cost per quality tier: CloudViewSampleCountScale (2) and CloudShadowViewSampleCountScale (1) set the volumetric sample counts; the Low and Medium precipitation tiers cap them (CloudLowViewSampleCap 1, CloudLowShadowSampleCap 0.5, CloudMediumViewSampleCap 1.5, CloudMediumShadowSampleCap 8 = no cap), and Low hides the cirrus dome unless bCloudLowCirrus is set.
  • Look tuning at runtime: SetCloudLookScalar(Param, Value) writes a cloud-material scalar (CloudBaseDarkening, CloudAzimuthAmount, StormGlowAmount, CloudStreetAmount, CloudSunTintAmount, …) through a transient dynamic instance — the asset is never touched.
  • UE 5.5–5.7 only (bFixVolumetricRTBeading, on): those engines' default quarter-resolution cloud reconstruction beads thin cloud filaments into dotted chains, so the Director switches r.VolumetricRenderTarget.Mode from 0 to 1 at BeginPlay when the project left it at 0 (about +3 ms per frame at 2560×1440). It does nothing on 5.8, where mode 0 renders clean.

Day & season length

Day length

DayLengthSeconds sets how much real time one in-game day takes. At runtime use SetDayLengthSeconds / SetDayLengthMinutes / SetDayLengthPreset rather than writing the property — the setters preserve the current time of day and change only the speed, where a raw write teleports the sun (the cycle’s phase is stored in seconds against the old length, so halving the day would jump the clock twelve hours). EBOSDayLengthPreset covers a real 24-hour day down to a one-minute day, and DayLengthForPreset exposes the numbers so a settings menu doesn’t have to duplicate them. ApplyHostOverrides takes it as a fourth parameter for lobby picks.

Season length

SeasonCycleDuration is how long one winter→summer sweep takes (a bouncing cycle returns to winter in twice that). At runtime use SetSeasonCycleDuration / SetSeasonCycleDurationMinutes: they preserve the season’s current phase — a raw write jumps the season, and the season drives the weather state and the landscape, so that jump is far more visible than a skipping sun. The setter also updates the authored-duration snapshot, or the next SetSeasonCycleRunning(true) would restore the level’s original length and throw the choice away. SetSeasonRandomSegments retunes the SM_Random wander instead; that mode replays deterministically from its seed, so changing it re-derives the sequence and the blend may step once. ApplyHostOverrides takes the sweep length as a fifth parameter.

The season cycle

With the winter pin off, the season axis drives the whole world — sky, ground, water, foliage and daylight — in this order as the blend rises from deep winter (0) to high summer (1) and back:

BlendWhat you see
0 – 0.2deep winter: full blanket, frozen ponds and creeks, snow falls (sleet band near 0 °C from ~0.2)
0.2 – 0.5spring: the blanket melts in islands (sun-facing slopes and edges first), prints and mounds fill fast, wet dark soil at the melt edge, the snow line rises, ice thins to slush and open water, trees bud (0.4+), precipitation turns to rain above +1 °C
0.5 – 0.9meadow: grass colours in, puddles collect in rain, footsteps switch to dirt / grass / splash, full canopy by 0.75, long high-sun days
0.9 – 1high summer
falling 0.8 → 0.3autumn: leaves turn then drop, the evergreens dull, first flurries whiten the ground, water bodies freeze back below −1 °C, days shorten

Everything here is deterministic: replicated season state plus world-space noise, no per-machine randomness, so two clients see the same melt pattern, the same puddles and the same leaf drop. A manual weather pick made while the season is sweeping holds the weather state only — the sweep itself (ground, ice, foliage, daylight) keeps running until the next season command.

What falls: snow, sleet or rain from the air temperature

A weather state says whether and how hard it precipitates; the air says what falls. With bPrecipTypeFromTemperature (on by default) it is snow below PrecipSnowBelowC (−1 °C), rain above PrecipRainAboveC (+1 °C) and sleet in between — both precipitation systems run at half density, surfaces get wet at half the rain rate, nothing accumulates. The type keeps its band until the temperature is PrecipTypeHysteresisC past the boundary, and weather zones resolve against the viewer-local temperature. So a Snowfall state in a warm spring falls as rain and melts the blanket, and a summer shower turns to snow on a cold mountain night. Freezing rain then needs rain or sleet at or below FreezingRainMaxAirTempC (+2 °C). Materials get PrecipIsSnow as 1 / 0.5 / 0. With the flag off, presets keep their authored type.

Foliage by season

Two scalars in MPC_SnowDeformation describe deciduous vegetation along the season axis: FoliageSeason (0 bare … 1 full canopy) and FoliageColorShift (0 green … 1 autumn colours). They are direction-aware: rising, trees bud around blend 0.4 and are in full leaf by 0.75; falling, they turn colour below 0.8 and drop their leaves between 0.55 and 0.30. Drop Materials/MF_SnowboundSeasonFoliage into your foliage master — feed it the leaf colour, opacity and a per-leaf random (PerInstanceRandom, a vertex colour, a texture) and take its Color / Opacity back. Leaves turn one by one and vanish one by one, with no texture work. A matching MF_SnowboundSeasonGround carries the ground side (snow → wet soil → grass) for host ground materials.

Daylight by season

bSeasonDrivesDaylight (off by default, on in the demo) shapes the day with the season: daylight lasts WinterDaylightHours (8) in deep winter and SummerDaylightHours (16) in high summer, centred on noon with the 24 h clock unchanged, and the noon sun climbs to SunNoonPitch × WinterNoonPitchScale (0.5) … SummerNoonPitchScale (1). Sunrise and sunset events, the twilight band and the night sky follow the real horizon crossings; GetSunriseHour / GetSunsetHour / GetDaylightHours report the current day.

Full cycle or winter-only cycle

By default the season drives everything: below blend 0.5 the ground is snow, from 0.5 to 0.9 the grass comes back, the ground-snow baseline follows the same curve (BOSSeason::GroundCoverageForBlend, also pushed as SnowCoverage / GrassCoverage to both material collections), accumulation thaws above 0 °C in every season and the summer half decays it to zero.

A game that wants “snow all the time” sets bWinterWeatherCycle = true on its director (or project-wide in DefaultGame.ini under [/Script/SnowboundWeather.BOSWeatherDirector]): the season loop then cycles only the sky and precipitation while the landscape stays at WinterGroundBlend (0 = deep winter). Temperature — and with it the geometric pile melt — always follows the real season. A snow shower in a warm season whitens the ground only in proportion to how wintry the season still is (SnowyWeatherFloor). The control panel’s “Season →” row toggles precipitation-by-temperature and the season-shaped daylight, and its Freeze / Thaw buttons force every placed water body solid or thaw all ice at once for testing.

Wind & realism

Engine wind (foliage)

The Director drives a UWindDirectionalSourceComponent from the live weather wind (bDriveEngineWindSource, on by default), so every SpeedTree / WPO foliage asset in the level sways with the storm and surges on gusts with no per-asset setup. If your level already places its own wind source, turn this off — the engine sums wind sources rather than replacing them. Assign GustCameraShake for a camera buffet on gust peaks (off by default; scaled by gust peak × storm severity, and client-local since the gust clock is already synced).

Smoothed direction changes

Raw wind targets can jump — a panel override, or a random per-state roll that lands near a reversal. With bSmoothWindChanges (Director, Wind › Realism, on by default) the displayed wind is a smoothed state instead: the yaw turns toward the target at WindTurnRateDegPerSec (25°/s ≈ a 7 s full reversal), strength ramps at WindStrengthRatePerSec, and every consumer — precip WindForce, foliage, cloud pan, snow drift, the upwind spawn-volume lead — reads the smoothed wind, so a direction change is a visible turn, never a snap. Three extras ride on it:

  • Reversal lull — while a swing bigger than WindLullStartAngleDeg is in progress, strength eases toward WindLullFloor (frontal-passage feel: the wind dies, veers, returns from the new quarter). WindLullFullAngleDeg sets where the floor is reached.
  • Idle meander — the settled wind wanders ±WindMeanderAmplitudeDeg on two slow sines (WindMeanderPeriodSeconds) driven by the replicated server clock, so it never holds a laser-straight heading and all machines wander in step.
  • Gust veer — each gust rolls a signed offset up to GustWindVeerMaxDeg from its seeded stream and leans the wind by it for the gust’s duration — gusts arrive slightly off the mean direction, not just louder.

Clients smooth the replicated target locally with the same constants, so no extra bandwidth is spent. Turn bSmoothWindChanges off to restore instant raw-target wind.

Drift-wind lag (deposited snow has inertia)

The smoothing above is the wind itself — particles, foliage and cloud pan should follow it within seconds. Ground snow drifts are deposited mass: when the wind veers, an existing lee pile must not swing around its obstacle in lockstep. With bLagDriftWind (Director, Wind › Realism, on by default) the snow kit feeds its drift material a separately lagged wind instead:

  • Direction creeps toward the live wind at DriftReorientRateDegPerSec × strength (default 1.5°/s ≈ a 2-minute full reversal in a gale) and stops entirely below DriftTransportThreshold — calm air moves no snow, so drifts freeze in place.
  • Amplitude builds with time constant DriftAmplitudeBuildTime (90 s) and decays with the longer DriftAmplitudeDecayTime (300 s), so piles linger after the wind dies.
  • DriftLiveWindFraction (0.15) mixes a sliver of the instant gust/breeze strength back in — a faint breathing instead of per-gust pile pulsing.

The knobs are pushed to the ground responder at BeginPlay (ConfigureDriftWindLag) with level-identical constants, so every machine lags in step. Turn bLagDriftWind off to restore drift piles that track the instant wind frame-for-frame.

Precipitation renderer

Two client-local settings decide how the falling snow and rain are drawn. Fog, overlays, squalls, audio and the snow kit’s accumulation are identical in every mode, so none of this changes gameplay or what other players see.

Mode

snowbound.Precip.Mode / SetPrecipRenderMode (static, Blueprint callable). Auto follows the engine’s Effects Quality (Low = screen-space sheet, Medium and up = world Niagara). World is the default Niagara look. Screen-space moves the falling snow and rain onto the lens post-process — the same M_LensPrecip blendable that draws drops on the glass also draws a three-layer parallax sheet of flakes and streaks, wind-slanted from the projected weather wind, with a whiteout veil at blizzard severity, each layer hidden where the scene is closer than its pseudo depth. That is one full-screen pass at any particle count and it is camera-agnostic, but flakes never pass behind a tree. Off draws nothing. Every world precip emitter the Director drives fades out over its particle lifetime while the sheet fades in (~1.5 s), so the mode can be switched mid-storm from an options menu.

Density and quality tier

snowbound.Precip.Density / SetPrecipDensity is one multiplier on the world spawn rate and on the screen sheet alike. <= 0 means Auto from the Effects Quality (Low/Medium ×0.5, High ×0.75, Epic and up ×1.0). Explicit values have no upper cap — the user decides between performance and quality.

snowbound.Precip.Tier / SetPrecipQualityTier sets how much and how good the world particles are. Auto follows the Effects Quality; Low / Medium / High set a density (×0.5 / ×0.75 / ×1.0) and a live particle budget (the Director thins the spawn rate so the falling population holds it); Cinematic has no caps (density ×1.5, unlimited budget, lit / collision / depth-of-field / motion-blur hints on) and lifts the Niagara quality level to Cinematic for the whole process, so engine scalability cannot thin the effects behind the user’s back; Custom uses the stored knob block (SetCustomPrecipKnobs). A tier can also select its own system set through the Director’s TierSystemOverrides map; unset slots fall back to the Auto* defaults, and a tier switch cross-fades the follow emitters.

The knob block reaches every managed system as the Niagara user parameters User.MaxParticles, User.SpawnRadiusScale, User.LitFlakes, User.Collision, User.DepthOfField, User.MotionBlur and User.CullDistance. Systems that do not declare them ignore them. The shipped systems read only User.Intensity and User.WindForce (plus the landed-flake handler); the hints are for systems you author or assign in TierSystemOverrides, so with the shipped systems a tier changes density, budget and the Niagara quality level.

Overrides and cost

PushPrecipOverride(Mode, Tier, Priority) / PushPrecipKnobOverride / PopPrecipOverride(Handle) let photo mode, cutscenes and Sequencer force a renderer and tier for a while without touching the user’s saved settings; highest priority wins, then the most recent. GetPrecipCostReadout() reports particles alive and systems active over the Director-managed components, the tier and knob block in effect, and whether the sheet is drawing (GPU time is not measured — use stat gpu). The demo panel prints it under the Precip row.

All of this persists in [SnowboundWeather] of GameUserSettings.ini (PrecipRenderMode 0..3, PrecipDensity, PrecipQualityTier 0..5, Custom*) and is re-read by every Director at BeginPlay. A game that attaches its own precipitation Niagara to the character must check ABOSWeatherDirector::IsWorldPrecipEnabled() in its precipitation-changed handler — the Director re-broadcasts that notify on every flip, and a system it does not own will otherwise keep raining in screen-space or Off mode.

The shipped falling and gust systems also carry the Niagara Effect Type NET_SnowboundPrecip, whose per-quality spawn-count scale (Low 0.4, Medium 0.6, High 0.8, Epic/Cinematic 1.0) applies even in projects that never touch these settings; it stacks with the density above. NET_SnowboundPrecip_Uncapped is the no-caps twin for Cinematic system sets.

SnowboundSnow module

The Deformable Snow Kit

Two cooperating systems, both driven by the weather and both deterministic in multiplayer:

Render-target deformation (UBOSSnowDeformationSubsystem, a world subsystem — no setup beyond materials): characters and objects stamp into a world-projected RT that your ground material samples — footprints, impact craters, slide trails, ragdoll craters, plus glazed-ice compaction, wind-drift scouring against an occlusion capture of your level, snowfall fill-in, and an exclusion mask (ABOSSnowExclusionVolume for interiors/overhangs). Characters get a UBOSSnowFootprintComponent attached automatically (bAutoAttachFootprints, on by default): every ACharacter without one receives one at spawn, with foot sockets auto-detected per skeleton (mannequin ball_l/r, foot_l/r, Mixamo *ToeBase/*Foot — a warning names the character when none match). A character that carries its own component (Blueprint or C++) is never touched; tag a character NoSnowFootprints to opt out, point AutoAttachFootprintClass at your configured Blueprint subclass for custom pools, or add the component by hand as before. The component does the rest: per-foot animation-driven stamps (socket names configurable), stance depth (crouch/sprint), per-surface footstep sounds/VFX (SurfaceFootstepData), deep-snow drag and ice friction (replicated so movement stays in sync), and a spawn-settle stamp. The optional IBOSSnowMoverState interface on the owning actor feeds game-specific state (sliding, rolling, sprinting, large prints); everything defaults sensibly without it.

Geometric snow piles (UBOSGeoSnowSubsystem): metaball deposits meshed with marching cubes grow real geometry on crates, roofs and props while it snows — fed by the falling-snow Niagara particles, so drifts appear where and when flakes land — and carve when characters wade or projectiles hit. Positions are a pure function of a replicated seed and the shared accumulation budget: every machine grows identical snow with zero per-deposit bandwidth. Buildup is coverage-first — early snowfall skins whole surfaces with a thin film that deepens together as the storm banks accumulation — and every fresh cap is born inside its receiver and rises out of it every frame (the emergence rise: the remesh writes a per-vertex grow age into the mesh's vertex colors and M_GeoSnow sinks/raises the surface from it, so nothing pops in and nothing steps). Snow reaches prop edges: each deposit near a drop-off measures a polar edge profile (bisection support traces per sector) and the field clips the kernel there with a rounded lip that overhangs a few centimeters, never cantilevers. Deposits under eaves and overhangs stay a dusting (sky-shelter probes), windward edges scour thinner, edges fill in last, and fresh ragged snow smooths as it settles. Melt follows the temperature field (a campfire melts a local hole; peaks stay white), winter thaw slowly decays piles in clear weather, and save/restore rebuilds the field from the seed. A season switch to spring or summer melts the piles the same way, on the Director's shared melt clock (SnowMeltCmPerDegSec, default 0.01 → ~3.5 min for a 45 cm pile at +22 °C; the kit's own MeltRadiusCmPerDegSec applies only without a Director or with bDriveGeoSnowMelt off): the piles thin out with the ground as its snow islands open (the pile budget follows the visible ground, see BudgetSource) and recede on the local temperature, nothing is deleted with the footprints. Cold peaks above the snow line keep theirs.

Which surfaces collect piles. Piles grow on components with Static mobility and collision (object type WorldStatic or WorldDynamic — immobile props left on WorldDynamic, such as roofs, count too). Anything movable neither receives piles nor shelters what is beneath it, because a moving occluder would give each machine a different layout; a door or physics crate stays bare, and a movable roof does not keep snow out of the room below. Opt an actor out with the NoGeoSnow tag (NoSnowTag); the same tag on a single component opts out just that component, e.g. one foliage type or a tree's canopy mesh. Painted foliage does not receive piles by default (bFoliageReceivesGeoSnow), because a tree's simple collision is one coarse hull over the crown; the canopy still shelters the ground beneath it. Nothing settles where the local temperature is above SettleMaxTempC (0.5 °C), e.g. inside a campfire's radius.

Ice: freezing-rain events stamp glaze during storms (replicated, with late-join replay), and level designers place ABOSIceSpot / ABOSIceSpline / ABOSIceArea actors for authored frozen ponds and paths; footfall compaction gradually polishes walked routes into slick ice.

Ice thaws, water freezes (season cycle): every ice pixel and the skate map thaw by IceThawPerDegSec (0.004 per °C above freezing per second — a glaze is a tenth of itself after a minute at +10 °C; 0 = the old permanent ice). Object snow tracks & physics grab

Add a Snowbound Object Snow Track component (UBOSSnowObjectTrackComponent) to any movable actor — a physics sphere, boulder, crate, vehicle — and it deforms the snow like a character does: a continuous berm-lipped furrow while it moves, a rest depression where it stops, and the carve is forwarded into the geometric prop snow automatically (rolling through a drift plows it). Track width auto-sizes from the collision bounds (TrackWidthScale); depth, speed scaling, stamp spacing and the elevated-structure gate are knobs on the component. Stamps only fire in actual ground contact, so thrown or carried objects never streak. Client-local cosmetics, same as footprints; it uses the batched stamp path, so many tracked objects stay cheap.

Physics grab (demo helper)

The Snowbound Physics Grab (Demo) component (UBOSPhysicsGrabComponent) lets a pawn pick up, carry and throw physics objects, the natural partner of the object snow track for testing: drag a crate through the blanket, throw it into a drift. It binds GrabKey (G, toggle) and ThrowKey (H) on the owning pawn by itself (bAutoBindKeys); turn that off and call TryGrab, Release, ToggleGrab and ThrowHeld from your own input. With bAutoEnablePhysics any movable prop becomes grabbable. Knobs: GrabRange (450 cm), HoldDistance (220 cm), MaxGrabMassKg (400, 0 = no limit), ThrowSpeed (1400 cm/s, mass-independent). Multiplayer: grabbing runs where physics is authoritative (standalone or the listen-server player). A grabbed prop starts replicating, so clients see it carried and thrown and stamp its tracks; clients cannot grab themselves (there is no client-to-server grab request).

Hooking up your ground material

Deformable snow in one node. Materials/MF_SnowboundDeformableGround is the demo ground's entire deformation response as a drop-in function — no reverse-engineering M_SnowGroundDemo any more. Nanite landscape displacement (experimental)

By default the ground deforms via WorldPositionOffset — supported everywhere and the recommended mode. LandscapeMode=1 renders the same height field through Nanite programmable tessellation displacement instead: real geometric detail at stamp edges instead of vertex-density-limited WPO. Flip it with the “Nanite Land” checkbox on the control panel, SetLandscapeMode(1), or the ini key; the choice persists per user.

Requirements (SetLandscapeMode(1) refuses with a log warning when unmet):

  • SM6 (DX12/Vulkan) on a Nanite-capable GPU — query IsNaniteLandscapeSupported().
  • The Landscape actor needs Enable Nanite checked and its Nanite data built (build-time data; the demo map ships that way).
  • r.Nanite.Tessellation=1 — the subsystem raises it for you and puts the previous value back when you switch off (set bManageTessellationCVar=false in the subsystem config if your project manages Nanite cvars itself). r.Nanite.ProgrammableRaster and r.Nanite.ComputeRasterization (default 1) must also be on; the log names the culprit if displacement stays invisible.

Why the switch also flips landscape.RenderNanite. UE hard-disables material WorldPositionOffset on landscape Nanite meshes (an engine rule, not ours) — so on a Nanite-built landscape, WPO snow can only render through the traditional landscape path. In Standard mode the subsystem therefore forces landscape.RenderNanite=0 (traditional path, WPO works) and in Nanite mode forces it back to 1 (Nanite mesh, tessellation displacement works), re-evaluating each landscape’s rendering method on the spot. The cvar is only ever touched in worlds that actually contain a Nanite-built landscape, and the value found beforehand is restored on teardown (bManageTessellationCVar=false opts out — but then Standard mode on a Nanite-built landscape will be flat unless you manage the cvar yourself).

How the switch works — one material, no swaps. Ground materials gate their two outputs on the SnowboundNaniteActive scalar in MPC_SnowDeformation (0 or 1, pushed by SetLandscapeMode):

WorldPositionOffset = <your WPO height vector> × (1 − SnowboundNaniteActive)
Displacement        = saturate(ComponentMask_Z(<same vector>) ÷ 128) × SnowboundNaniteActive

The demo ground material is the worked example. Because the Displacement scalar derives from the Z of the final WPO vector, both modes always show the identical height field (carve, piles, wind drift, berms). The ÷128 matters: the engine saturates the Displacement input to 0–1 before scaling, so a raw centimeter height would clamp to a featureless slab — normalize over your full height range (the kit uses 128 cm). Then set Used with Nanite, Enable Tessellation and Displacement Scaling: Magnitude is in the mesh’s local units, so full-scale displacement of 128 cm on a landscape with Z-scale S needs Magnitude = 128 / S (the demo ships the value computed for its own landscape; Center stays 0 — tune Magnitude to taste). A ground material without the gate simply keeps its WPO path while the mode is active — nothing breaks; it just doesn’t tessellate.

Known engine caveats (why this stays opt-in): UE 5.8 tessellation is itself experimental — the subsystem defuses a known render-thread crash by zeroing the gate and rebuilding landscape render state at world teardown, and stamp edges can show faceting on tessellated landscapes (an engine artifact; a softer deformation-RT mip for the displacement sample is the workaround if it bothers you).

Slope-aware coverage (steep ground sheds snow)

Real snow does not stay on steep ground. On smooth terrain the holding capacity collapses between about 45° and 60° (the SnowSlide field model: 1.1 m at 45°, 0.45 m at 60°, 5 cm above 75°), a thin dusting still sticks to much steeper rock, and rough faces keep snow on ledges to 70–80°. The demo ground material models exactly that from the landscape’s vertex normal — no extra textures, no baked masks, identical in Standard and Nanite mode:

  • Holding window — full blanket up to SnowSlopeFullDeg (45), bare rock from SnowSlopeBareDeg (60). The threshold is jittered by world-space noise (SnowSlopeNoiseDeg, SlopeNoiseScaleCm) so the snow line never reads as a cut, even on coarse 1 m landscapes whose vertex normals carry no micro-relief.
  • Dusting — while the blanket is thinner than DustingDepthCm, the bare angle sits at SnowDustingHoldDeg (75) and retreats to 60° as the storm deepens it; thaw brings it back out. A first snowfall whitens the cliffs, a deep blanket slides off them.
  • Benches — contour-aligned ledges (BenchSpacingCm, BenchStrength) keep snow striped across faces past the bare angle, the way real rock faces stay white to 80°.
  • Wind aspect — lee slopes hold more, windward slopes are scoured (SlopeWindBias), from the same lagged drift wind the wall drifts read, so a wind reversal re-sculpts the hills over the smoothed turn.
  • Rock — albedo and roughness fade to a procedural rock look (SteepRockColor, RockVariation, RockRoughness on the material instance) SlopeColorFeatherDeg before the height reaches zero; footprints, berms and the deformation normal all scale with the holding factor, so nothing carves or shades on bare rock.
  • Snow line (Director, Snow Line category, off by default) — with bDriveSnowLineFromTemperature the Director derives the altitude where its lapse-rate temperature model crosses SnowLineTemperatureC (sea level = the Director’s Z, see AltitudeLapseRateCPerKm) and the ground below that line sheds its snow across SnowLineBandCm; a season or weather change moves the line at SnowLineSmoothingRate instead of snapping it. bSnowLineOverride / SetSnowLineOverride pin it to a world Z.
  • Sun-aspect melt (Director, AspectMeltStrength, off by default) — slopes facing the sun lose their cover first once the air at the viewer is warmer than AspectMeltStartC, reaching full strength AspectMeltRangeC above it. Night or a sun below the horizon melts nothing. Both terms derive from replicated or level-static inputs, so every machine agrees.

The slope knobs live on UBOSSnowDeformationSubsystem (Config, Slope section; SetSnowSlopeAngles at runtime, also on the control panel) and are pushed to MPC_SnowDeformation — like the sampler knobs they must match across machines. The Director pushes the snow line, sun direction, air temperature and melt knobs into the same collection through the ground responder (SetTerrainMeltParams), so a ground material binds one collection. The CPU follows the same model: GetSlopeHold (slope only) and GetTerrainHold (slope × snow line × sun aspect), GetSnowDepthAtPositionOnSlope, GetVisualSnowHeightAtOnSlope. The footprint component feeds the movement component’s cached floor normal into them, so on a rock face (or below the snow line) there is no drag, no muffled footstep and no print, furrow, crater or trail (SlopeStampHoldMin); the plain GetSnowDepthAtPosition keeps its flat-ground meaning but does respect the snow line.

Your own ground material. Evaluate the holding factor in one Custom node from VertexNormalWS and WorldPosition (vertex-shader legal: no ddx/ddy, no PixelNormalWS) and multiply your base rise, pile and berm terms by it; lerp albedo/roughness to your rock by 1 − hold; in the Nanite branch keep the × saturate(VertexNormalWS.z) factor described above. For the snow line multiply by smoothstep(SnowLineZ − SnowLineBandCm, SnowLineZ + SnowLineBandCm, WorldPosition.z) (skip when SnowLineZ ≤ −1e8), for the sun aspect by 1 − saturate(dot(N, SunDirToSun)) × saturate((AirTemperatureC − AspectMeltStartC) / AspectMeltRangeC) × AspectMeltStrength. The demo master is the worked example (nodes tagged SnowboundSlope*). An MPC_SnowDeformation copied before these scalars existed keeps working slope-blind, with one log warning naming what to add.

Shipped defaults. Effects/NS_SnowPuff (a soft snow-dust puff that rises, spreads and settles) and Effects/NS_SnowSpray (kicked-up clumps thrown along the travel direction, plus a little dust) are one-shot bursts — their emitters loop once, so the pooled component is released the moment the last particle dies. Runtime/DA_SnowInteractionVFX is a ready-made preset wiring them into every interaction (puff on each footstep, spray on sprint kicks / plowing / slides / landings / impacts, everything scaled by strength and spaced by travel). It is applied automatically: every footprint component whose VFXPreset is empty uses the subsystem’s DefaultVFXPreset (empty = this asset), so adding the component is the only setup. Assign your own preset per component (it always wins), point DefaultVFXPreset at yours in DefaultGame.ini ([/Script/SnowboundSnow.BOSSnowDeformationSubsystem]), or set bUseDefaultVFXPreset=false there to opt out. The effects are lit and deliberately restrained — white dust over sun-lit snow reads as a haze — so raise Scale / CountMin / CountMax in a copy of the preset if you want more. Both systems are built from Epic’s Niagara module scripts only and read no user parameters; the component still pushes Strength / SnowDepth / Velocity for systems that do.

  • OnWeatherStateChanged / OnPrecipitationChanged (C++ + BP) — bind gameplay or audio to state and precipitation edges instead of polling.
  • Director BP events: OnThunderStrike(Location, Intensity) (server-rolled, synced, with distance-delayed audio), OnFreezingRain, OnWeatherGust, OnWeatherParamsUpdated.

User settings (GameUserSettings.ini)

The snow kit reads a [SnowboundSnow] section from GameUserSettings.ini, designed to be written by an in-game options menu: SnowDeformation (0=off, 1=2K, 2=4K, 3=8K RT), SnowAccumulation (0/1), SnowReactionRate (0–3 → footprint tick rate), SnowVFXScale (0..4 multiplier on every snow-interaction Niagara count; 0 = none), LandscapeMode (0=WPO, 1=Nanite displacement — experimental), plus hand-tuning keys StampIntensity, RTWorldSize, StampEdgeSoftness.

Multiplayer notes

All state mutators are server-authoritative (client calls are ignored with a warning). Late joiners reconstruct weather/time/season/wetness and replay recent ice events. Cloud-shape overrides, cloud placement, the accumulated cloud drift and the sky speed replicate, so every machine renders the same deck in the same place. Squall banks replicate as spawn records; positions derive from the synced server clock. Geometric snow accumulation is deterministic per plugin version: run the same plugin build (and the same snow.Geo.* tuning values) on every machine in a session, or pile layouts will diverge between host and clients.

Known limitations

  • No splitscreen: viewer-local effects (zones, squall culling, splash anchoring) assume a single local player.
  • Dedicated servers run the state machine, severity and replication but skip all cosmetics (sky driving, audio, VFX).
  • Ground deformation is projected straight down (world X/Y): prints stretch on very steep slopes, and walls or the underside of overhangs cannot carry them. The geometric prop snow is real geometry and has no such limit.
  • Physics grab is host-only: standalone or the listen-server player grabs; clients see the result but cannot grab.
  • Blueprint-only projects need any C++ class before packaging, so the game target links the plugin's modules (see Packaging under the Snow Kit).
  • Nanite landscape displacement (LandscapeMode=1) is experimental and off by default: UE's tessellation is itself experimental (teardown crash defused by the subsystem, edge faceting possible — see the Nanite section above). The standard WPO path is the supported mode.
  • No audio assets ship. The Director exposes five ambience slots (wind, rain, drip, winter, summer) and the footprint component a per-surface footstep table — assign your own sounds; everything is silent until you do. The ground-state pools (water, wet ground, bare ground) are silent until filled too.
  • Bare ground is procedural. The demo ground material paints soil, grass and puddles from world-space noise and colour parameters — no grass or soil textures ship. Drive your own ground textures from the SnowCoverage / GrassCoverage / GroundWetness scalars and the ice render target's R (ice) and G (water) channels.
  • Winter-only projects set bWinterWeatherCycle (see "Full cycle or winter-only cycle"): ground, ice thaw, water bodies, temperature-resolved precipitation and foliage all hold their winter values; only the weather states cycle.
  • The rain ripple decal was authored with Substrate enabled; it is verified working in a non-Substrate project, but if your project renders it oddly, please report it.
